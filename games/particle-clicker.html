Instructions

Click the detector in the middle of the screen to collect data. Every click produces a small amount, and at the start that is your only income - so the opening few minutes are genuinely just clicking while you accumulate enough to buy the first useful thing.

Spend data on research from the left panel, and reputation on staff from the right. Research unlocks upgrades and converts into reputation; reputation lets you hire people who generate data without you clicking. The whole game is a loop between those two panels, and the point at which hired staff out-earn your clicking is the point it becomes an idle game rather than a clicking one.

Do not neglect upgrades. The Upgrades panel multiplies what everything else produces, and it is easy to pour everything into hiring more people while ignoring the multipliers that would make the people you already have worth several times as much. As a rule, buy an upgrade whenever you can afford one without stalling your next hire.

Tips to Master Particle Clicker

Particle Clicker was made during CERN Webfest, a summer hackathon where students working at the laboratory spend a weekend building things that communicate science to the public. That origin explains the game's unusual shape: it is a genuine incremental game rather than a quiz with a score attached, but the progression is mapped onto how a real physics collaboration actually works - you need data, and to get data you need instruments, and to get instruments you need a reputation that attracts funding.

The discoveries are real

Each research item you unlock corresponds to an actual result in particle physics, with a short description you can read once you have bought it. Working through them takes you roughly along the history of the field, from early detector work up to the Higgs boson. It is a light touch - nobody is going to learn physics from a clicker game - but the descriptions are accurate and it is a much better use of the genre than the usual cookies or paperclips.

How the incremental loop works

The genre convention is that manual input gets you started and automation takes over. Here the two currencies keep that from being a single straight line: data comes from clicking and from staff, reputation comes from completing research, and staff cost reputation rather than data. So a run has a rhythm - click up enough data to fund research, cash the research into reputation, spend reputation on people who then click for you - rather than one number climbing forever. It also means you can stall by over-investing in one side.

It keeps running while you are away

Once you have staff, the laboratory produces data whether or not the tab is open, and the game saves your progress to your browser's local storage. That makes it a reasonable thing to leave in a background tab and check on occasionally rather than something to sit and grind at. Because the save lives in your own browser, clearing site data will reset it, and progress will not follow you to a different computer.

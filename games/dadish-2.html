Instructions

Move with WASD or the arrow keys and press Space to jump; pressing Space a second time while airborne triggers a double jump, which is required to clear several gaps that a single jump falls short of.

Because Dadish can only move and jump — there is no attack button — obstacles are solved by pushing crates onto switches, riding moving platforms, and bouncing off springs rather than fighting through them.

Some sections use environmental hazards like fire that damage on contact and screen-shake effects tied to specific triggers, so watching for a hazard's activation cue before committing to a jump through that area avoids a blind hit.

Tips to Master Dadish 2

Since the double jump only fires from a second Space press while still airborne, pressing it too early (right after leaving the ground) wastes the extra height before it is needed, while pressing too late after the fall has already started limits how much height it adds back.

No attack means every obstacle is a puzzle

With only movement and jumping available, a blocked path in Dadish 2 is solved by finding the crate to push, the switch to hit, or the platform to ride — not by fighting past it. Looking for an interactive object near a dead end is more productive than trying to jump around it.

Watch for hazard activation cues

Environmental hazards like fire and shaking platforms tend to have a visible tell just before they trigger, so pausing at a safe distance to watch one cycle through its pattern once is faster overall than jumping in blind and losing progress to a hit.

About Dadish 2

Dadish 2 is the sequel to Thomas K. Young's platformer about a radish dad and his runaway children. Once again Dadish has to track down his kids, this time across new worlds full of fresh hazards, enemies and boss fights.

The core is the same as the first game: short, tightly designed levels where you can only run and jump, a hidden star in every level, and a boss at the end of each world. Dadish 2 adds new obstacles and level ideas on top of that, along with the same deadpan jokes that made the original popular. If you enjoyed Dadish, this is more of what worked, with a bit more challenge.

Instructions

Obstacles damage your shields and running out of shields ends the run, so survival is the first priority on every screen. You avoid them by clicking or tapping cards in your hand, and the most basic cards in the deck are the red, green and blue lane changes that shift you out of the path of whatever is coming.

Every card costs energy, tracked by the meter in the bottom right, and a card you cannot pay for cannot be played. That budget is the real difficulty: the deck usually contains an answer to what is in front of you, and the question is whether you can afford it right now or need to survive on a cheaper option and save up.

Minerals collected during flight are spent between planets, where you buy new ability cards and ship upgrades, sometimes from decidedly sketchy characters. A deck that is too large dilutes the cards you actually need, so buying selectively beats buying everything you can afford.

Controls

  • Click / tap a card — spend energy to trigger that ability
  • Lane cards — the red, green and blue cards move you between lanes
  • Shop screens — buy new ability cards and upgrades between planets

Tips to Master SHUTTLEDECK

Endless runners are about reaction speed and deck-builders are about deliberate planning, and SHUTTLEDECK works because the cards impose a rhythm on the runner - you are always deciding which resource to spend rather than how fast you can twitch. It is an unusually ambitious structure to attempt at this size, since it needs an economy, a shop and a card system all inside the same 13 kilobytes.

Energy discipline beats card collection

New players tend to spend energy on the first card that answers a threat, then find themselves with a full hand and an empty meter when a harder obstacle arrives. Reading one or two obstacles ahead and taking the cheapest survivable option is what carries runs, and it is the reason a small efficient deck usually outperforms a large one.

Designed for a phone as well as a laptop

Wexler built the game to be desktop and mobile friendly from the start, and the card interface is why that works - there is no virtual joystick or precise input to fake on a touchscreen, just cards large enough to tap accurately. It plays identically on a school Chromebook and a phone, which is rarer than it sounds for an action game.

Instructions

Press enter to start, move with the arrow keys, jump with Z and hold X to run. The physics are noticeably different from the NES games: Mario is lighter, the jump arc is flatter, and defeated enemies explode rather than being stomped flat, so timing learned from Super Mario Bros needs adjusting.

There are twelve levels spread across four worlds, which is short by Mario standards, but the stages are longer and denser than the count suggests. Each world closes with a boss, and the game is designed to be finished in a sitting - it was a handheld launch title meant to be played in short bursts on a bus.

Worlds two and four replace the platforming with side-scrolling shooter stages where Mario pilots a submarine and then a plane, firing with the same button he normally uses to run. They come without warning and catch most first-time players out, so expect the rules to change twice before the end.

Tips to Master Super Mario Land

Nintendo R&D1 was Gunpei Yokoi's hardware-focused division, the group behind the Game Boy itself, and handing them Mario produced a game with a genuinely different sensibility. The enemies are unfamiliar, the music by the R&D1 team sounds nothing like Koji Kondo's work, and the setting was invented wholesale. It is the closest the mainline series ever came to a spin-off.

Princess Daisy starts here

Daisy was created for this game as the ruler of Sarasaland, kidnapped by the alien Tatanga rather than by Bowser, who does not appear. She went on to become a fixture of the Mario sports and party games decades later, but her entire original characterisation is contained in this one Game Boy cartridge.

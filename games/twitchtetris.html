Instructions

Move with the left and right arrows, soft drop with down and hard drop with space. Rotate counter-clockwise with Z and clockwise with X or the up arrow. Press C or Shift to put the current piece in the hold slot and take out whatever was there - a mechanic the original Tetris did not have and which changes strategy completely.

Hold a piece you cannot use yet rather than forcing it. The usual application is banking an I piece while you build a flat surface with a well down one side, then dropping it for a four-line clear. Hold can only be used once per piece, so it is a genuine decision rather than a free undo.

Tune the key repeat if you play seriously. The Controls Options screen exposes both the initial delay before a held direction starts repeating and the repeat rate itself, which default to 200 and 50 milliseconds. Lowering them makes pieces slide across the board faster and is the single biggest difference between casual and competitive feel.

Controls

  • Left / Right arrows — move the piece sideways
  • Down arrow — soft drop
  • Space — hard drop - slam the piece down instantly
  • Z / X / Up — rotate - Z counter-clockwise, X or Up clockwise
  • C / Shift — hold the current piece for later

Tips to Master TwitchTetris

Pieces are dealt from a shuffled bag containing exactly one of each of the seven tetrominoes, refilled when it empties. That is the modern guideline standard, and it matters enormously: it guarantees you can never wait more than twelve pieces for an I piece, which makes building a well a reasonable strategy rather than a gamble. Older Tetris games used unbiased random and could starve you for far longer.

Wall kicks make rotation forgiving

When a rotation would put a piece inside a wall or another block, the game tries a series of small offsets before rejecting the move outright. That is what allows the T-spins and tight fits that modern Tetris play is built around, and the offset tables are written out explicitly in the game's source. It is why a piece sometimes slots into a gap that looks too narrow for it.

Scoring rewards patience and streaks

Clearing several lines at once is worth disproportionately more than clearing them one at a time, and clearing lines on consecutive placements earns more again. The practical consequence is that stacking flat and waiting for a four-line clear outscores playing safe, provided you do not top out doing it - which is the central risk-and-reward decision the game keeps asking you to make.

Why it looks like a terminal

The presentation is a deliberate joke about the game's own engineering focus - monospaced green text, a help panel written as a man page, and the actual JavaScript source rendered as wallpaper behind the board. Everything is drawn with plain HTML5 canvas, which is why it starts instantly and runs smoothly on hardware that struggles with heavier browser games.

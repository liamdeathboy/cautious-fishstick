Instructions

Walk with the arrow keys, jump with Z and crack the whip with X. Once you leave the ground your trajectory is fixed - no mid-air adjustment at all - so every jump has to be correct before you make it. That is not a bug or a limitation of the era; it is the constraint the whole game is balanced around.

Collect hearts and use sub-weapons. Hearts are not health, they are ammunition for the throwing weapons you find in candles - the axe, the holy water, the cross, the dagger and the stopwatch. Hold up while attacking to use whichever you are carrying, and note that picking up a new one replaces the old one.

Holy water is the answer to most bosses. It burns on the ground where it lands and locks enemies in a damage loop, and combined with the double or triple shot upgrade it trivialises several fights that are otherwise very hard. If a boss is stopping you, going back for holy water is more effective than practising the fight.

Controls

  • Arrow keys — walk, crouch and climb stairs
  • Z — jump - and you cannot change direction in mid-air
  • X — crack the whip
  • Up + X — use the sub-weapon you are carrying
  • Enter — START - begin and pause

Tips to Master Castlevania

Modern platformers let you correct a jump mid-flight. Castlevania does not, and that turns every gap and every staircase into a commitment. It makes the game feel heavy and deliberate rather than floaty, and it means enemies placed to knock you off ledges - particularly the Medusa heads - are a genuine threat rather than an annoyance. Learning the game means learning to look before you leap, literally.

Knockback into pits

Taking a hit sends Simon backwards, and most deaths in the game are falls caused by damage rather than by running out of health. The consequence is that clearing enemies before committing to a jump is nearly always worth the extra seconds, and that a full health bar does not mean you are safe on a narrow ledge.

Six stages and a run of famous bosses

The castle runs through six blocks ending in the Grim Reaper and then Dracula himself, with the Phantom Bat, Medusa, the Mummies and Frankenstein's Monster along the way. Death on stage five is widely considered harder than Dracula, and arriving at that fight without holy water is the point most runs end.

Music that outlived the console

Vampire Killer, Stalker and Wicked Child are among the best-known pieces of NES music ever written, and Konami's sound team pushed the hardware hard to get them. The soundtrack has been rearranged in nearly every subsequent Castlevania game, which is unusual persistence for a set of tunes composed for two pulse channels and a triangle wave.

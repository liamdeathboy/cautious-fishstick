Instructions

Move with the arrow keys or WASD and jump with Z or the space bar. The camera sits behind the Captain in true 3D rather than the flat side-on view most 13 kilobyte entries settle for, so watch the shadow beneath him to judge where a landing will actually put you before committing to a jump.

The jetpack is the mechanic that defines the game. Hold X or shift to fire it and gain height in mid-air, which turns almost every gap into a question of fuel management rather than timing. A jump you have already committed to badly can often still be rescued with a short burst, so reach for the jetpack rather than accepting the fall.

Press R at any point to restart the current level instantly, M to toggle the music, and escape to return to the main menu. The quick restart is there because the game is built around learning a level's layout through repetition - failing a section, seeing exactly what caught you out, and clearing it on the next attempt.

Read the shadow, not the character

The most useful habit in any third-person 3D platformer is to track the shadow rather than the character. Depth is genuinely ambiguous from a camera sitting behind the player, and the shadow is the only element on screen that tells you which platform you are actually above. Players who miss jumps here are almost always misjudging depth rather than distance.

Jetpack fuel is the real resource

Because the jetpack can extend or rescue nearly any jump, the interesting decisions are about when not to use it. Burning fuel early to clear a gap you could have jumped normally leaves nothing for the recovery you will need thirty seconds later, and since R restarts a level instantly, the cheapest way to learn a section's fuel budget is simply to run it a few times and watch where you run dry.

Instructions

Hold A or the left arrow to drift left, D or the right arrow to drift right — there is no jump, brake, or speed control, so every obstacle has to be dodged sideways while the ball keeps accelerating down the track.

Red blocks, narrow rails, and sudden gaps appear with no warning beyond what is already visible ahead, so react to what is on-screen rather than trying to memorize a fixed layout since the course is procedurally generated and different every run.

Because speed increases continuously, a lane you can drift across safely in the first thirty seconds may require a much earlier steering commitment later in the same run — the correct reaction distance keeps shrinking.

Controls

  • Right / D — drift right

Tips to Master Slope

Slope gives you exactly two inputs — left and right — and no way to slow the ball down. When a red block or gap appears, the only option is committing to a lane change early enough to clear it; hesitating and drifting halfway is what causes most falls, since the ball will still be centered over the hazard when it arrives.

Track the edges, not just the obstacles

The slope has a limited width, and the ball can roll off the side just as easily as it can hit a red block. Chaining several steering corrections in the same direction to dodge one obstacle can carry you off the track entirely, so every dodge needs a matching correction back toward center.

Expect the run to get harder, not the pattern to repeat

Because the course generates new terrain as you go and speed rises the longer you last, there is no fixed sequence of obstacles to learn by heart. Runs end when reaction time stops keeping pace with the current speed, so the practical skill being built is faster recognition, not memorization.

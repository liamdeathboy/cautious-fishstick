Instructions

Use the arrow keys to drive forward, backward, and steer; the left and right keys also tilt the buggy while airborne, which is how flips get performed off jumps rather than through any separate dedicated flip button.

Score comes from three sources at once: successfully landing flips off high jumps, collecting stars scattered through each level, and completing the level quickly, so a run focused purely on speed leaves flip and star points unclaimed.

Power-ups scattered through levels — growth, shrink, slow-motion, and single-wheel effects — change handling in ways that can help or hurt depending on the specific obstacle ahead, so grabbing every power-up without considering the upcoming terrain is not automatically beneficial.

Controls

  • Arrow keys — drive and balance the buggy
  • Space — jump over obstacles
  • Shift — boost for short bursts

Tips to Master Dune Buggy

Because tilt input during a jump is what actually performs a flip, waiting until the buggy is already falling to start tilting leaves too little airtime to complete the rotation cleanly — starting the tilt input as soon as the buggy leaves the ground gives the most time to land the flip before touching down.

Scoring rewards balance across speed, stars, and flips

Since finishing quickly, collecting stars, and landing flips all contribute to score independently, a run that only optimizes for one of the three usually scores lower overall than a run that spends a little extra time landing flips and grabbing stars along an otherwise efficient route.

Power-ups can hurt as easily as help

Because power-ups like shrink or single-wheel change handling in ways that are not universally beneficial, grabbing one immediately before a section that specifically needs stable, full-size handling can make that section harder rather than easier — reading the upcoming terrain before deciding whether to grab a nearby power-up avoids an unhelpful handling change at the wrong moment.

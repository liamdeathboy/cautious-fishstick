Instructions

Move and climb with the arrow keys or WASD, jumping with up, W or space. The Super Meat Boy lineage shows in the handling - the ninja is fast, wall-jumps are central to vertical progress, and levels expect you to chain movement rather than take each jump as a separate considered decision.

Getting spotted is the fail condition, not taking damage. That changes how you approach a room: the question is never whether you can survive crossing it, but whether anything has line of sight while you do. Waiting a beat for a guard to look away is almost always cheaper than trying to outrun detection.

Every level has a straightforward path and at least one more difficult route that saves time, and the on-screen timer exists so you can chase those. The intended arc is to clear a level however you can first, then come back and find the faster line once you know where the sightlines are.

Tips to Master Ninja vs EVILCORP

Unlike stealth games that track sound, suspicion meters and alert states, this one reduces the genre to a single readable rule - can something see you right now. That is partly a byte-budget decision and partly what makes it work as a platformer: you can evaluate a room at a glance and commit to a run through it, which keeps the pace much closer to Super Meat Boy than to a conventional stealth game.

Two routes per level, by design

Vansteelandt has said the levels were deliberately built with obvious routes alongside more difficult alternates. That structure is what gives a short jam game replay value - the first clear teaches you the room, and the second run is about spotting the wall-jump chain or the gap in patrol timing that the safe route was letting you avoid.

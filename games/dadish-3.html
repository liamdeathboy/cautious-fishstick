Instructions

Move and jump with the same controls as earlier Dadish games, but watch for two new level types introduced here: Momato levels, where you ride a character with enhanced jump height and distance, and Dolphin the Dolphin levels, which auto-scroll forward and force you to dodge obstacles to keep pace rather than moving at your own speed.

New mechanics like claws and pipes appear alongside the returning crate-pushing and switch-hitting puzzles from earlier games, so a level's solution may now depend on interacting with one of these new elements rather than only the classic double-jump-and-push toolkit.

Collecting every star across all 50 levels unlocks Panic Mode, a bonus mode rewarded specifically for full completion rather than just finishing the main story, so partial star collection leaves that mode locked even after beating final boss Lord Kanrud.

Tips to Master Dadish 3

Because Dolphin the Dolphin levels auto-scroll forward regardless of player input, falling behind the scroll speed ends the attempt the same way missing a jump would — these levels require committing to obstacle dodges in real time rather than the more deliberate, unhurried pacing that earlier Dadish games and most of this one's other levels allow.

Momato's enhanced jump changes what a level expects from you

Since Momato levels give access to a jump with more height and distance than Dadish's own, level design in those sections is built around gaps and obstacles that assume the enhanced jump is available — treating a Momato level like a standard Dadish level underestimates what the enhanced jump is actually meant to clear.

Panic Mode is a completionist reward, not a story requirement

Because Panic Mode only unlocks after collecting every star across all 50 levels, beating Lord Kanrud and completing the story is possible without ever seeing it — treating star collection as optional busywork means missing content that specifically rewards full completion.

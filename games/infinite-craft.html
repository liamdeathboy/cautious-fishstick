Instructions

Drag an element from the sidebar onto the board, then drop a second element on top of it. If the pair has a recipe, they merge into something new which is added to your list permanently. Water and earth give you a plant, water and wind give you a wave, and from those four starting tiles the tree opens outward.

Use the search box once your collection grows. After an hour you will have hundreds of elements in the sidebar and scrolling for the one you want becomes the slow part, so filtering by name is the difference between experimenting freely and hunting through a list.

Combining an element with itself is a legitimate move and often the productive one when you are stuck - water on water gives a second water tier, which then combines with things the first could not. Doubling up is the standard way to break out of a dead end.

Tips to Master Infinite Craft

Neal Agarwal's original Infinite Craft is genuinely open-ended because it asks a language model to invent a result whenever it meets a pair it has not seen, which requires a live server. This build ships a fixed recipe table instead, so its tree is finite and identical every time you play. That trade buys something the original cannot offer: it runs entirely offline, on any locked-down network.

A finite tree is a different game

Because the recipe list is fixed at 508 combinations, this version has an actual completion state - there is a definite set of 458 things to find rather than an endless generated space. That makes it closer to a collection puzzle than a sandbox, and the interesting question shifts from what will it invent to what have I not tried yet.

Start broad, then go deep

The efficient opening is to combine all four starting elements with each other in every pairing before chasing any single chain, because those six results are the roots almost everything else grows from. Players who immediately follow one promising line tend to stall, while players who widen their base first find the later branches open much faster.

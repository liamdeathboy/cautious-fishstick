Instructions

Use WASD or the arrow keys to move and press Space to jump; pressing Space again while airborne triggers a double jump, the only way to clear several of the wider gaps in later levels.

Since Dadish cannot attack, obstacles are solved entirely through movement and interaction — pushing crates onto pressure switches, riding moving platforms, and bouncing off springs are the tools available for every puzzle in the game.

Because this is the first game in the series, its levels only use the basic tools of moving, jumping, crates, springs and moving platforms, which makes it the best place to learn how Dadish handles before trying the sequels.

Tips to Master Dadish 1

Because Dadish has no way to fight back against anything, a blocked path is always solved by finding the crate to push, the switch to hit, or the platform to ride rather than by defeating an enemy — looking for an interactive object near a dead end is the correct instinct whenever progress seems blocked.

The double jump has to be timed, not just pressed twice quickly

Since the second jump only adds height if pressed while Dadish is still rising from the first jump, pressing it too early wastes some of the extra height while pressing it during the fall limits how much height gets added back — the timing of the second press matters as much as remembering to press it at all.

This is the simplest entry in the series mechanically

Because later Dadish games add new interactive elements — claws and pipes in Dadish 2, Momato and Dolphin levels in Dadish 3 — this original entry is built entirely around the core movement-jump-push toolkit without any of those later additions, making it the most straightforward starting point in the series.

About Dadish 1

Dadish is a 2D platformer by indie developer Thomas K. Young. You play Dadish, a radish dad whose many children have wandered off, and you travel through dozens of short, colourful levels to bring them all home.

Levels are bite-sized and packed with hazards: spikes, moving platforms and walking junk-food enemies like burgers and fries that hurt on contact. Each level also hides a collectible star for players who want more of a challenge, and bosses wait at the end of each world. The game's charm comes from its jokey writing and cute pixel art, but its precise platforming can get properly tough later on, which is why it led to several sequels.

Dadish 1 FAQ

Who made Dadish?

Dadish was made by indie developer Thomas K. Young, who also created its sequels Dadish 2 and Dadish 3.

Can Dadish attack enemies?

No. Dadish cannot fight, so every enemy and hazard has to be avoided by moving and jumping carefully.

Is Dadish good for kids?

Yes. It has cute art, gentle humour and no violence, though later levels need quick reflexes.

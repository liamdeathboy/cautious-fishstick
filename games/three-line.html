Instructions

The board is a grid of tiles, each showing a number in its own colour. Use the mouse to select and move them; the objective, as the title states, is getting three into line. Matching runs clear from the board and the grid refills, in the pattern the whole tile-matching genre inherited from Bejeweled and its ancestors.

Because each number also has a distinct colour, you can scan the board by either property. That doubles the number of patterns you can spot quickly - looking for three of a colour is faster in a crowded grid, while looking for the digit is more reliable when several tiles share a similar hue.

Ruffle asks you to click once before the game starts, which is a browser requirement rather than part of the game. If the frame looks like a static play button, click it and the Flash content will begin; nothing else about the original behaviour is changed.

Tips to Master Three Line

When Adobe discontinued Flash in December 2020, tens of thousands of browser games became unplayable overnight. Ruffle is a Flash Player reimplementation written in Rust that runs the original files directly, so what you are playing here is the actual game as it shipped rather than someone's approximation of it - bugs, timing quirks and all.

Colour and number carry the same information

Pairing every digit with its own colour is redundant encoding, and redundancy is exactly what makes a match puzzle readable at speed. It also means the board stays playable for anyone who has trouble separating particular hues, since the number is always there as a second channel telling you what a tile is.

Runs entirely from this site

The Flash runtime is served from Schplay rather than pulled from a third-party CDN, which matters on networks that block outside script sources. Everything the game needs - the emulator, its WebAssembly core and the .swf itself - loads from the same origin, so it behaves the same on a locked-down school connection as it does at home.

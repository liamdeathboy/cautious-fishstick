Instructions

Left-click a tile to reveal it; revealing a mine ends the run immediately, while revealing a safe tile shows a number (or nothing, if no adjacent tile has a mine, which automatically reveals the surrounding blank area too).

Right-click a tile to flag it as a suspected mine, which prevents an accidental left-click reveal on tiles you have already deduced are dangerous; flagging is optional but becomes necessary for tracking deductions on a larger board.

A revealed number tells you exactly how many of its neighboring tiles (including diagonals) contain a mine, so if a "2" tile already has two flagged neighbors, every other neighboring tile is guaranteed safe to reveal.

Controls

  • Mouse — reveal tiles or place flags
  • Space — chord-click to clear safe squares
  • F2 — start a new board instantly

Tips to Master Mine Sweeper

Because a revealed number counts precisely how many of its up-to-eight neighbors are mines, once that many neighbors are already flagged, every remaining unrevealed neighbor of that tile is provably safe — this is the core deduction that clears most of a board without guessing.

Blank-tile chains reveal information for free

A revealed tile with no adjacent mines shows no number and automatically opens all of its neighbors too, cascading until every tile bordering the open area at least shows a number. Clicking near the edges or corners of a board first tends to trigger larger chain reveals than clicking near the center.

The endgame sometimes forces a genuine guess

Because mine placement is fixed at the start but not fully knowable from the numbers alone, boards occasionally reach a state where two or more unrevealed tiles are equally likely to hold the last mine — recognizing when a position is a real 50/50 rather than continuing to search for a nonexistent logical certainty saves time on the final tiles.

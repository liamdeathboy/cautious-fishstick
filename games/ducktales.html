Instructions

Press enter to start, move with the arrow keys, jump with Z and use X for the cane. The pogo is the technique that matters: hold down while airborne and press the cane button to bounce, which lets Scrooge cross spikes and enemies that would otherwise be lethal and gains height with each successful bounce.

There are five stages - the Amazon, Transylvania, African Mines, the Himalayas and the Moon - and you may tackle them in any order from a world map. That structure was unusual for the time and rewards knowing which stage's treasure makes the others easier before you commit.

The cane also serves as a tool on the ground: swing it at loose blocks and chests to clear paths and uncover money. Since your final score is Scrooge's fortune, exploring for hidden treasure is not optional flourish but the actual objective the ending is graded on.

Tips to Master DuckTales

Most platformers of the era gave you a jump and an attack as separate ideas. DuckTales fuses them - bouncing on the cane is simultaneously how you kill things, how you cross hazards and how you gain height, which means skilled play looks nothing like walking. Players who never learn the pogo find the game punishing; players who do rarely touch the ground.

Why the Moon theme outlived the game

Hiroshige Tonomura worked at Capcom for only two years, from 1988 to 1990, and the music he wrote for the fifth stage has long outlasted that tenure. The Moon theme has been rearranged, covered and remixed continuously for over three decades, and it is routinely cited among the finest compositions ever written for the NES sound hardware.

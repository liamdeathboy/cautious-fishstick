Instructions

Move with the arrow keys, jump with Z and attack with X, then press Enter to start. The attack button is contextual - a normal punch connects as a punch, but the final hit of a combo turns your fist into something absurd and does far more damage, so finishing combos matters more than mashing.

Grab enemies and throw them. Walking into a stunned enemy and pressing attack picks them up, and thrown bodies damage anything they hit. In the beat 'em up stages this is more efficient than trading blows, because it removes two enemies from the screen with one action and leaves you out of counterattack range.

Level three is the Turbo Tunnel and it is where most runs end. You ride a speeder bike down a corridor of walls that must be dodged by memorised timing, at a speed that steadily increases. There is no trick beyond repetition - the wall pattern is fixed, so treat each attempt as learning the next few seconds of the sequence.

Tips to Master Battletoads

Battletoads refuses to stay a beat 'em up. Across its run it becomes a vertical rope descent, a speeder-bike race, a surfing level, a snake-riding climb and a sequence where you outrun a giant rotating drill. Each one arrives with its own control conventions and almost no tutorial, which is a large part of why the game is hard: you are constantly a beginner again.

The two-player mode has a famous flaw

Two players can fight side by side, and friendly fire is on - you can and will hit your partner, who is stunned by it. Worse, the original cartridge has a well-documented bug that hangs the game in two-player mode on the Wookie Hole stage, which is why serious attempts at finishing Battletoads have historically been solo runs. Play co-op for the early stages and the chaos, not for a completion.

Difficulty as a design decision

Rare built Battletoads with limited continues and no password system, in an era when rental shops meant a short game was a commercial risk. The result is a title that is genuinely unfair in places and genuinely brilliant in others, and its reputation has outlived most of its contemporaries. Playing it in a browser with save states available is, frankly, a kinder experience than anyone had in 1991.

What to expect from the first three stages

Ragnarok's Canyon opens the game gently as a straightforward brawl with a descent on a rope, and it exists to teach you the combat rhythm. Wookie Hole follows, a vertical abseiling stage where you rappel down a shaft dodging obstacles and gravity does most of the work against you. Then comes the Turbo Tunnel, and the difficulty jumps so sharply that many players in 1991 never saw a fourth stage at all. If you want to see the rest of the game, the honest advice is to use the emulator's save state feature at the tunnel entrance rather than restarting from the beginning every time.

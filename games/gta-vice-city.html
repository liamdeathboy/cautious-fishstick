Instructions

You play Tommy Vercetti, dropped into a 1986 Miami-style city after a drug deal goes wrong. Follow the pink and yellow map markers to start story missions, or ignore them entirely and free-roam — steal cars, cause chaos, buy properties, and build a criminal empire at your own pace.

The first time you launch the game it downloads the world data once (this is a large open-world title), then caches everything in your browser so later sessions start much faster. A short intro plays before the main menu — press a key or tap to skip.

Missions fail if Tommy dies or is busted, or if a mission-critical vehicle or character is destroyed. You respawn at the nearest hospital or police station with your weapons gone, so keep some cash in reserve and grab body armor before hard missions.

Tips to Get Ahead in Vice City

Vice City rewards preparation over reflexes. Before a shooting mission, drive past a clothing store or your safehouse to pick up armor, and park a fast, healthy car right outside the mission trigger so a botched getaway is still recoverable.

Buy property to unlock save points and income

Story progress opens up businesses you can purchase. Each one becomes a save point and pays out a small daily income you collect by walking over the cash icon, so buying property early snowballs into a steady bankroll for weapons and repairs.

Lower your wanted level instead of outrunning it

Police pressure ramps up fast. Drive into a Pay 'n' Spray to instantly clear stars and repair your car, or grab a police bribe star pickup. Trying to simply outdrive a four-star chase almost always ends with the car wrecked.

Who made this browser version?

Grand Theft Auto: Vice City was released by Rockstar Games in 2002. This in-browser version is a community WebAssembly port built on the open-source reVC reverse-engineered engine (the re3/miami project) via reVCDOS — it ships no original Rockstar assets and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Rockstar Games or Take-Two Interactive.

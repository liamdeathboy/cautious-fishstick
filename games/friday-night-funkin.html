Instructions

Press the arrow keys or WASD to match each colored note as it slides up and lines up with the matching arrow at the judgment line near the top of the screen; hitting a note late or early scores lower than hitting it exactly on time.

Missed or badly-timed notes drain a health bar shared between Boyfriend and his opponent, visualized as a tug-of-war icon between the two characters — enough missed notes in a row loses the song outright rather than just lowering the score.

Songs are grouped into weeks, each introducing a new opponent with a different note pattern and tempo, so a note-reading rhythm built up against one opponent's song does not automatically transfer to the next week's different rhythm and speed.

Controls

  • Arrow keys — hit notes on beat
  • W / A / S / D — alternate controls for lefty players
  • Enter — pause and swap songs

Tips to Master Friday Night Funkin

Since the health system is visualized as a struggle between Boyfriend and the opponent rather than a bar that only drains, hitting notes well actively pushes the balance in your favor while missing them pushes it back — recovering from a bad section is possible if enough following notes land cleanly.

Each week resets note-pattern familiarity

Because every week introduces a new opponent with distinct song patterns and tempo, timing muscle memory built against one week's songs does not carry over cleanly to the next — treating the first attempt at a new week as a learning pass rather than expecting immediate mastery matches how the difficulty curve is actually structured.

Early or late hits both count against accuracy

Because the judgment line rewards precise timing rather than just eventually pressing the right key, hitting consistently early (anticipating the beat) or consistently late (reacting after it passes) both score lower than hitting exactly on the line — adjusting timing based on which direction your misses lean fixes more accuracy than simply pressing faster or slower overall.

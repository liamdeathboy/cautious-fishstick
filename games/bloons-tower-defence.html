Instructions

Click a tower in the Build Towers panel and place it next to the path. Five types are available - dart tower, tack shooter, ice tower, bomb tower and super monkey - each with a different range, rate of fire and cost. Towers only attack what passes within their radius, so placement matters more than raw quantity.

Learn the bloon hierarchy. Red bloons are the weakest; blue move faster and contain a red inside; green contain a blue; yellow are faster still; and black bloons are small, slower than yellows, and contain two yellows each. Popping a layer reveals what is inside it, so a wave of blacks is far more work than the count suggests.

Upgrade rather than sprawl. The dart tower's piercing upgrade lets each dart pop up to two bloons instead of one, which effectively doubles that tower's output for far less than a second tower costs. Concentrating upgrades on well-placed towers beats scattering cheap ones around the map.

Tips to Master Bloons Tower Defense

The ice tower freezes nearby bloons, which sounds strictly good until you read the detail: frozen bloons are immune to darts and tacks, and only bombs will destroy them. Placing ice next to your dart towers can therefore make your defence worse rather than better. It is the most interesting decision in the game and the one new players most reliably get wrong.

Placement beats purchase

The strongest positions are on the inside of tight corners and next to sections where the path doubles back, because a tower there covers two stretches of track at once. A cheap tower in a corner often out-performs an expensive one on a straight. Before buying anything, look at the path shape and find the spots where a single circle of range overlaps the most track.

Where the series went

Ninja Kiwi built this out of an earlier Flash game called Bloons, in which you simply threw darts at balloon arrangements. The tower defence version arrived in 2007 and the series has run through six numbered sequels and a large mobile business since, adding monkey specialisations, heroes and towers far beyond these five. The core loop has barely changed.

Money in, lives out

Every bloon that reaches the end costs you lives, and every one you pop pays money. That makes the difficulty curve self-correcting in an unusual way: a leak is not just damage, it is lost income that would have funded the towers you need for the next round. Early rounds are where a run is won, because that is where compounding starts.

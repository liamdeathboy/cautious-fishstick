Instructions

Press the space bar to start running and to jump. Hold it slightly longer for a higher jump - the height scales with how long the key is held, which matters once cacti start appearing in clusters of three that need a full-height leap to clear.

Press the down arrow to duck. Pterodactyls appear later in a run and fly at three different heights: the low ones must be jumped, the high ones can be run straight under, and the middle ones require a duck. Reading their height quickly is the main thing separating a score of 500 from a score of 2000.

You can also press down while in the air to drop faster. This is the technique that most people never discover, and it matters at high speed - a fast landing after a jump gets you back in control before the next obstacle, where a floaty arc would carry you into it.

Tips to Master T-Rex Runner

The run has no levels and no end. Speed increases continuously with distance, and at intervals the palette inverts into a night mode with white obstacles on black. The switch is purely cosmetic but it is disorienting the first few times, and it arrives around the same distance that pterodactyls start appearing, which is why so many runs die shortly after it.

Where the dinosaur came from

The offline page's dinosaur was drawn by Sebastien Gabriel, a Chrome designer, and the team has described it as a deliberate nod to prehistory - a joke about connections going extinct. The pixel style was chosen partly because the error page had to stay tiny and could not depend on loading external assets, which is the same constraint that makes it work well on a blocked school network.

You do not actually need to be offline

In Chrome you can reach the same game any time by typing chrome://dino into the address bar, which opens it in a full window rather than squeezed onto an error page. That version and this one are the same code. Running it here simply means you do not have to leave the page you are on, and it works in browsers other than Chrome.

Scoring, and what a good run looks like

Score is simply distance, ticking up continuously, with a high score stored in the browser and shown as HI at the top right. There are no points for near misses and no bonus for style. A first attempt usually ends somewhere under 500, a comfortable player sits around 1000 to 2000, and past that the speed is high enough that cactus clusters and pterodactyls arrive together and reaction time stops being sufficient on its own. Beyond roughly 1500 the game stops adding new obstacle types and only increases speed, so the ceiling on a run is your reflexes rather than anything the game introduces.

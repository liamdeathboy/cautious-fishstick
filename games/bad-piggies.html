Instructions

Bad Piggies flips the Angry Birds formula: instead of launching a bird at pig fortresses, you're helping the pigs by building a vehicle for them. Each level gives you a limited set of parts — wheels, motors, balloons, propellers, fans, umbrellas, and TNT among others — that you drag onto a chalkboard chassis to construct something that can get a pig from the start of the level to the finish flag.

Once you launch your build, it's live physics: engines apply force, balloons lift, TNT explodes on impact or on command, and your contraption has to survive ramps, gaps, and obstacles using only the parts you attached before launch. Every level can be solved multiple different ways.

Each level scores up to three stars based on performance — usually a mix of finishing the level, collecting the crates scattered along the route, and completing an optional time or distance challenge — so a build that merely reaches the finish isn't always a three-star build.

Controls

  • Mouse — drag parts onto the chassis to build, then click to launch
  • Mouse click — activate motors, balloons, TNT, and other parts during the run

Tips to Master Bad Piggies

Balance thrust against weight. A contraption with too many heavy parts and not enough motors or balloons will crawl or tip over, so add lift or propulsion before adding more structural pieces.

Star crates change the ideal build

Since crates are often placed off the direct path or high in the air, a vehicle built purely to reach the finish quickly may need reworking with balloons or a detachable piece to also grab every collectible.

TNT is a tool, not just an obstacle-clearer

Timed correctly, an onboard TNT block can launch your contraption over a gap or ramp it upward, not just blow apart something in your way — factor it into your route, not just your defenses.

Cheap, simple builds often outscore complicated ones

Because every extra part adds weight and cost, the three-star solution to a level is frequently a small, well-balanced vehicle rather than the biggest contraption the parts budget allows.

Instructions

Press spacebar or click to flap — each press applies the same fixed upward velocity regardless of how the bird is currently moving, so tapping rapidly near the top of the screen will send you climbing past pipes instead of leveling off, while tapping too rarely lets gravity build up speed on the way down.

The bird falls under constant gravity between flaps, so the game rewards a steady rhythm of small taps over frantic mashing; watch the rotation of the bird sprite as a visual cue for your current fall speed.

Every gap between the top and bottom pipe is the same fixed width, so once you find the tap-timing that clears one pipe cleanly at your current speed, the same rhythm clears the next one — the difficulty comes from pipes appearing back-to-back, not from the gap size changing.

Tips to Master Flappy Bird

Every tap gives the bird the exact same upward kick no matter its current speed, so the skill is entirely about timing when you tap, not how hard or how long you hold. Tapping while already rising sends you higher than intended; the reliable pattern is to let the bird dip slightly below a pipe gap's center line before tapping again.

Aim for the gap center, not the pipe edge

Because both pipes in a pair are set a fixed distance apart, threading the vertical center of the gap gives you the most room for error on the next flap. Players who hug the top or bottom pipe run out of correction room if the next gap shifts height.

The common death is over-tapping near a pipe

The most frequent mistake is panic-tapping as a pipe gets close, which launches the bird into the top pipe instead of threading the gap. Since gravity is constant and predictable, a single well-timed tap from a stable falling position clears more pipes than several rushed ones.

About Flappy Bird

Flappy Bird was created by Vietnamese developer Dong Nguyen and released on phones in 2013. In early 2014 it became one of the most downloaded games in the world, and then Nguyen removed it from the app stores, saying it had become too addictive. Since then countless browser recreations have kept the game alive, and this is one of them.

The rules have never changed: every tap gives the bird a small upward hop, gravity pulls it back down, and you score one point for each pair of green pipes you pass. Touching a pipe or the ground ends the run immediately. There are no power-ups, no levels and no second chances, which is exactly why a score of 10 feels like an achievement and a score of 50 feels heroic.

Flappy Bird FAQ

Who made Flappy Bird?

The original Flappy Bird was made by Dong Nguyen and published by his studio .GEARS in 2013. This page hosts a browser recreation of that game.

Why is Flappy Bird so hard?

The gaps between pipes are small and every flap gives the same fixed boost, so there is no room for panicked tapping. Players who score high keep a calm, steady rhythm and aim for the middle of each gap.

Can I play Flappy Bird at school?

Yes. This version runs in any modern browser, including school Chromebooks, with no download.

Instructions

Every node you own accumulates units over time, shown as a filling arc around it. Drag from one of your nodes to another to dispatch exactly half of its garrison along that link, and note that a node has to be holding at least twelve units before it is allowed to send anything at all. Nodes fill to a ceiling of 360, so a full one sitting idle is wasted production.

Distance costs you units, but only when you attack. Sending to a neutral or enemy node loses a share of the force in transit that scales with how far apart the two nodes are, while reinforcing one of your own nodes arrives at full strength no matter how far it travels. That single asymmetry is what makes short, local attacks and long, safe reinforcement the correct shape of most winning games.

An attack resolves as a straight comparison of the numbers that actually arrive. More than the defender and you capture the node with the surplus as its new garrison; fewer and you simply reduce it while it holds. An exact tie is reported as a draw and leaves the node empty for either side to take, which is usually the worst outcome available to you.

Controls

  • Drag between nodes — send units from one of your dots to another
  • Click / tap — select a node and choose menu options
  • VOL + / - — adjust the generated music and effects volume

Tips to Master Konnekt

Five tutorials lead into named boards - Easy, Circles, Ring, Close, Poles, Unfair, Frenzy and a randomised layout. What changes as you climb is the rate at which nodes generate units, which starts at zero in the tutorials and rises steeply by the time you reach Unfair and Frenzy. The opponent does not get cleverer, it gets richer, so those levels are won on tempo - striking during the window before its production advantage compounds.

The sphere is the whole design

On a flat conquest map you can see every threat at once. On a globe you cannot, and Konnekt uses that constantly - the Poles and Ring levels are built specifically around positions that are adjacent on the sphere but appear far apart from a given camera angle. Rotating to check the far side before committing units is not a convenience here, it is a core part of playing well.

Half-strength attacks are the common mistake

Because a send dispatches only part of a node's garrison, launching two attacks from the same node in quick succession usually delivers two forces that are each too small to take anything, while leaving the origin node stripped and vulnerable. Waiting for one node to fill and sending a single decisive wave beats trickling units at a target almost every time.

Sound that is generated, not recorded

Each level assigns different synthesised instruments to you and to the opponent - piano, bells, organ, strings and flute across the level list - so the note you hear tells you whose move just resolved without your having to look. It is also a practical necessity: with a 13 kilobyte budget and no audio files allowed, every sound in the game has to be produced by code at the moment it plays.

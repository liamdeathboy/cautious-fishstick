Instructions

Run and aim with the arrow keys, fire with S, jump with X and grab enemies with Z. Before the first stage the game asks you to choose Fixed or Free shot: Fixed locks you in place while firing so you can aim precisely, and Free lets you run and shoot at once. Free is easier to pick up; Fixed is what most experienced players end up using.

Combine your weapons. You carry two of four base guns - Force, Lightning, Chaser and Flame - and holding both together produces a different weapon entirely rather than just switching. Chaser plus Lightning gives homing lightning; Flame plus Chaser gives tracking fire. Experimenting with pairs is most of the game's depth, and some combinations are dramatically stronger than the guns they are made from.

Do not forget you can grab. Melee is unusually good here - you can seize most ordinary enemies and throw them, slide into them, or drive them into the ground, and doing so costs no ammo and often clears a crowd faster than shooting. Players who treat this purely as a shooter miss roughly half of the moveset.

Tips to Master Gunstar Heroes

Four base weapons, held two at a time, and the ability to merge whichever pair you are carrying gives a set of distinct combined weapons rather than a simple upgrade ladder. What makes it work is that the combinations are not just stronger versions - they behave differently enough to change how you approach a stage. Chaser combined with Lightning produces a homing beam that trivialises some bosses and is nearly useless against others, so the choice you make at a weapon pickup is a real decision rather than a reflex to grab the newest thing.

Bosses that come apart

Treasure built its reputation on boss design and this is where it starts. Gunstar Heroes' bosses are large multi-part constructions that lose pieces as you damage them and change behaviour as they do, so a fight has visible phases you can read off the boss itself rather than off a health bar. Seven Force, a robot that transforms through a series of animal shapes mid-fight, is the one everyone remembers, and the studio reused the idea in later games including Alien Soldier and Radiant Silvergun.

Treasure's debut

Treasure was formed by former Konami staff who wanted to make original games rather than licensed sequels, and Gunstar Heroes was their first release. It is unusually confident for a first game - the sprite counts, scaling effects and sheer amount happening on screen were pushing the Genesis harder than most of its library, and it established the house style the studio kept for a decade. If you like this, Alien Soldier and Dynamite Headdy are the obvious next stops on the same hardware.

The dice maze

Partway through the game sits a board-game stage where you roll a die and move around a grid of squares, each triggering a fight, a shop or a trap. It has essentially nothing to do with the rest of the game and is the sort of thing that gets cut from most projects. Its survival here is a fair summary of Gunstar Heroes as a whole: the game is full of ideas that a more disciplined studio would have removed, and it is much more memorable for keeping them.

Instructions

Move the paddle with the mouse or the left and right arrow keys, and press space to launch. The ball's angle off the paddle depends on where it hits - striking it near the edge sends the ball off sharply, while the centre returns it steeply. That is the only aiming control you have, and it is the whole skill of the game.

Work the ball into the gap behind the wall. The classic technique is to break a channel up one side so the ball gets trapped between the bricks and the ceiling, where it clears rows on its own while you wait. It is far more efficient than picking bricks off from below and it is the strategy the original arcade cabinet was designed to reward.

Expect the ball to speed up. Breakout traditionally accelerates after a set number of hits and again when you reach the higher rows, and the paddle often shrinks partway through. Anticipating where the ball will be, rather than chasing where it is, is the adjustment the later stages demand.

Tips to Master Breakout

Atari commissioned Breakout as a single-player answer to Pong, and Steve Wozniak designed the original circuit with a famously small number of chips while Steve Jobs handled the contract. It shipped in 1976, and a few years later the same basic idea - a paddle, a ball and a wall - had spawned an entire genre. Very few games have that direct a line of descent.

The angle off the paddle is everything

Because the paddle imparts angle based on contact point, a good player is not simply returning the ball but aiming it. Deliberately catching the ball near the paddle edge to steer it toward a specific column is how you carve the channel up the side, and it turns a reaction game into something closer to a very fast game of snooker.

Why the genre never died

Brick breakers persisted through Arkanoid's power-ups, DX-Ball's shareware era, and the modern ball-count variants where you fire dozens of balls at numbered bricks. The core keeps working because it is legible instantly and has a skill ceiling well above where most players stop - the same reason Tetris and Pong are still around.

Running as plain JavaScript

This version needs no Flash, no plugin and no download - it is HTML5 canvas and JavaScript, which is why it starts instantly and works on any modern browser including on a phone. Recreating Breakout is a common exercise for people learning game programming, which is why open-source versions like this one are plentiful and often carry a permissive licence.

Instructions

Move the mouse to aim your crosshair and click to fire; unlike a keyboard-only shooter, aim is entirely analog through mouse position rather than snapping to discrete lanes or targets.

Press R to reload once your shots for the current wave run out — missing shots still counts against your limited ammo, so firing at a duck that has already changed direction wastes a shot that a wave often cannot afford to lose.

Press Space to start the next wave once the current one clears; ducks tend to move faster and change direction more abruptly as waves progress, so a shot pattern that worked on an early wave may not track a later wave's movement.

Tips to Master Duck Hunt

Because ducks change direction abruptly and the crosshair takes a moment to move with the mouse, clicking where a duck currently is often fires behind where it has already moved. Aiming slightly ahead of a duck's flight path lands more hits than tracking its exact current position.

Ammo is limited per wave, not per shot

Since each wave gives a fixed number of shots before requiring a reload, missing early in a wave leaves fewer shots to clear the remaining ducks. Waiting half a second for a clean, close shot is usually worth more than firing early at a duck still far away or moving unpredictably.

Later waves need a faster aim update, not just faster clicking

As waves progress, ducks move faster and change direction more abruptly, so the skill that scales is how quickly the aim adjusts to a duck's new heading, not how quickly the mouse button is clicked. A rushed shot on a wrong prediction wastes ammo that a later, corrected shot could have used instead.

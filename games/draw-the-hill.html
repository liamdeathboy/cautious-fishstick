Instructions

Draw a line from the car's starting point with the mouse or your finger to create the road it will drive on; the car follows whatever path gets drawn, so steering the car is really about drawing the correct path rather than controlling it directly.

Steep uphill sections slow the car down and can stall its momentum entirely if drawn too aggressively, while downhill sections accelerate it — drawing a small ramp just before a jump adds extra speed compared to launching from a flat line.

Bumpy or jagged lines can flip the car outright, so drawing smooth, deliberate curves rather than shaky or overly detailed lines keeps the car stable across the drawn road.

Controls

  • Mouse / Touch — draw slopes for the sled
  • R — reset the run instantly
  • Space — start the vehicle down your track

Tips to Master Draw The Hill

Because there is no separate steering or acceleration input, every aspect of the car's behavior — speed, jump height, stability — is determined entirely by the shape of the line drawn, so thinking of the drawing itself as the actual gameplay input, not just a visual layer, is central to improving.

Slope angle trades speed for control

Since steeper inclines produce faster movement but less control while gentler slopes offer more control at lower speed, matching the steepness of a drawn section to what is actually needed next (a burst of speed for a jump versus careful control near an obstacle) works better than drawing uniformly steep or uniformly gentle lines throughout.

Smooth lines prevent avoidable flips

Because bumpy or jagged lines can flip the car outright regardless of how well the overall route is planned, drawing deliberately smooth curves — even if it costs a moment of extra care — avoids losing a run to an unstable road surface rather than to an actual obstacle.

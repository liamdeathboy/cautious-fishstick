Instructions

Player 1 moves with the arrow keys, shoots with Z, and drops bombs with X; Player 2 moves with WASD, shoots with T, and drops bombs with Y — up to four players can join using additional numpad control schemes.

Shots and bomb blasts add knockback to whoever they hit, so a rival does not need to lose health directly — enough accumulated knockback from repeated hits eventually sends them flying off the platform for an instant knockout.

Jumping and dodging matter as much as shooting, since standing still to line up a shot exposes you to being knocked off from an angle you were not watching — the platform's edges are the real threat, not a health bar reaching zero.

Tips to Master Gun Mayhem

Because each successful shot adds to a rival's accumulated knockback rather than a separate health pool, a player who has already taken several hits can be launched off the platform by a much weaker follow-up shot than the ones that came before it — tracking how much knockback an opponent has already taken matters more than any single hit's strength.

Bombs threaten the platform, not just the opponent

Since bombs create area knockback rather than a direct-hit shot, they punish an opponent who is standing still near the platform edge more severely than one who keeps moving, making them most effective against a player who is trying to line up a precise shot of their own rather than staying mobile.

Platform position beats aim precision alone

Because ring-outs happen from position relative to the platform edge rather than health depletion, staying near the center of the platform while forcing opponents toward the edges wins more exchanges than trading shots evenly from a position already near an edge yourself.

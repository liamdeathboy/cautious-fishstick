Instructions

Running trains speed: click to make your duck jump over obstacles that appear along the track, with each successful jump improving how far your duck can run without tripping.

Swimming trains a separate stat using the up arrow to jump and the down arrow to dive underwater around obstacles, while Flying uses the arrow keys to steer altitude up and down through a series of aerial hazards.

Races test all three stats at once rather than just one, so training only the minigame you find easiest leaves a duck with weak stats in whichever area got ignored, which shows up directly as a weaker placement in races that emphasize that skill.

Controls

  • Arrow keys — train each discipline
  • Mouse — feed your duck and buy seeds
  • Space — jump during competitions

Tips to Master Duck Life 1

Unlike later entries that add features like Climbing (Duck Life 2) or breed evolution (Duck Life 3), the original Duck Life trains exactly three stats — Running, Swimming, and Flying — through their respective minigames, with race performance depending on a balance across all three rather than any single specialized stat.

Balanced training beats maxing one skill early

Because races draw on running, swimming, and flying stats together rather than testing them in isolation, a duck trained heavily in one minigame while neglecting the others tends to lose races that emphasize the neglected stat, even with an otherwise strong overall duck.

Prize money funds the farm, not just future races

Since race winnings go toward rebuilding the family farm as the game's overarching goal rather than purely toward more training, treating early races as a means to bankroll the farm's reconstruction — not just as practice for later, harder races — matches how the original game frames its progression.

Instructions

Click the paddle onto the ball to start a point, then move the mouse to control paddle position, returning the ball before it passes your side of the court.

Striking the ball while the paddle is in motion imparts curve based on the paddle's direction and speed at the moment of contact — the faster the paddle is moving on impact, the more pronounced the curve on the resulting shot.

Because curve is tied specifically to paddle motion at contact rather than paddle position alone, a hit from a paddle that is stationary at the moment of contact returns the ball in a straight, predictable line regardless of how the paddle moved beforehand.

Controls

  • Mouse — move the paddle and apply spin
  • Click — serve the ball when ready
  • Space — pause between rounds

Tips to Master Curveball

Because only the paddle's speed and direction at the exact instant of contact determines curve, moving the paddle rapidly before the hit but slowing to a stop right at contact produces a straight shot — deliberately timing paddle motion to peak speed at the moment of impact is what actually generates a strong curve.

Curved shots are harder to read, not just harder to reach

Since a curved shot bends its path in flight rather than traveling in a straight predictable line, an opponent positioning based on the ball's initial trajectory can still miss it as the curve takes effect later in its path — this makes curve a way to beat a well-positioned opponent, not just a way to reach a wider area of the court.

Straight shots still have their place

Because a stationary-paddle hit is fully predictable but also fast and simple to execute, mixing occasional straight shots with curved ones prevents an opponent from anticipating curve on every single return, keeping them uncertain about which type of shot is coming next.

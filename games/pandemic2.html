Instructions

Choose one of three pathogen types — virus, bacteria, or parasite — at the start, since each has different base mutation rates and available traits, then spend evolution points on transmission methods (air, water, blood, or animal-based spread) and symptoms.

Symptoms both help and hurt your spread: visible symptoms like coughing or vomiting make infected people easier to spot and quarantine, while symptoms that increase lethality too early can kill hosts before the disease spreads further, so timing symptom upgrades relative to transmission upgrades matters.

Adjust the in-game time controls to slow down or speed up simulation while you plan the next round of upgrades, and use the tutorial and in-game guide if a specific mechanic like resistance research or government response is unclear.

Tips to Master Pandemic 2

Because visible symptoms make an outbreak easier for governments to detect and quarantine, prioritizing transmission methods before adding highly visible or lethal symptoms lets the disease spread to more countries before serious containment measures can catch up to it.

Lethality timing determines whether hosts survive to spread further

Since a disease that kills its hosts too quickly loses its ability to spread through them, holding back lethality-boosting traits until transmission has already reached most of the world's population is usually more effective than maximizing deadliness from the very first evolution points spent.

Resistances counter the response, not the population

Because world governments research vaccines and treatments in response to a spreading outbreak, investing evolution points into drug and heat/cold resistances specifically counters that response rather than helping the disease spread further on its own — these traits matter most once containment efforts are already underway.

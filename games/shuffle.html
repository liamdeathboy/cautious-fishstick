Instructions

Click one of your red marbles and drag the mouse to set both the throwing angle and the power behind the shot; release the mouse button to launch it toward the yellow marbles.

Clearing every yellow marble off the board before the computer clears all of your red ones advances you to the next level, which then gives you fewer marbles to work with than the level before.

Rebound physics off the board's edges let a single well-aimed shot bank off a corner and take out multiple yellow marbles in one throw, rather than needing a separate shot for each one.

Controls

  • Mouse — flick pucks across the table
  • Space — change aim angle precisely
  • P — pause to analyze opponent moves

Tips to Master Shuffle

Because rebounding off a corner or edge can redirect a marble into a second or third yellow target after the initial hit, lining up a bank shot that clears several yellows at once is far more efficient than aiming directly at one yellow marble per throw, especially as levels give fewer marbles to work with.

Throw strength needs calibration, not just aim

Since overshooting sends your own marble past the target (and potentially off the board, wasting it), tweaking throw strength to match the actual distance to a target — rather than always throwing at maximum power — avoids losing marbles to overshoot, which matters more as later levels give fewer marbles to spare.

Fewer marbles per level raises the cost of a wasted shot

Because clearing a level reduces the marble count for the next one, a throw that misses or overshoots becomes proportionally more costly on later levels than it was early on — later levels reward more deliberate aiming and power control than the more forgiving early levels required.

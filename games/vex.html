Instructions

Pick a specific entry from the hub — Vex 3, 4, or 5 — since each is a separate set of levels with its own trap layouts, even though the core controls stay consistent across the series.

Use the arrow keys or WASD to move and jump, and hold down (or S) while running to slide under low obstacles; holding down while stationary at the start of certain sections can also trigger a level to begin.

Later levels combine spikes, lasers, and moving platforms in tighter sequences than earlier ones, so a run that cleared early obstacles by reacting in the moment may need more deliberate, planned movement once traps start overlapping.

Tips to Master Vex's Games

Because the arrow-key/WASD movement and slide mechanic stay consistent from Vex 3 through Vex 5, comfort with the controls transfers immediately between entries, but the specific placement of spikes, lasers, and platforms is unique to each game — expecting a later entry's levels to resemble an earlier one's is a common way to misjudge a jump or slide timing.

Sliding is for clearance, not just speed

Since holding down while running triggers a slide specifically meant to pass under low obstacles, using it purely as a speed technique without checking whether an obstacle actually requires it can mistime a jump that was needed instead — reading the specific obstacle ahead determines whether a slide, a jump, or neither is the right response.

Overlapping traps in later levels need pre-planned movement

Because later levels combine hazards more tightly than the earlier, more spaced-out ones, treating every obstacle as something to react to individually — rather than reading a whole sequence ahead of time — becomes less reliable as traps start overlapping, making planned movement through a full sequence more important than pure reflexes.

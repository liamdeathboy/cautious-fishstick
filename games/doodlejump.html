Instructions

Hold the left or right arrow key to drift your doodle across the screen; it wraps around the edge instantly, so cutting through the left wall to reach a platform on the right (or vice versa) is often faster than steering the long way around.

Press spacebar to start the run or restart after a fall — there is no separate jump button, since the doodle bounces automatically every time it lands on a platform.

Watch the platform color as you climb past score thresholds: past 100 points moving platforms start appearing, past 500 points breakable platforms (which give way after one bounce) enter the mix, and past 1,000 points vanishing platforms show up alongside them, so the higher you climb the less you can trust any single platform to still be there next time.

Controls

  • Left / Right Arrow — drift the doodle sideways; the screen wraps at both edges
  • Space — start the run or restart after a fall
  • Landing on a platform — bounce automatically — there is no jump button

Tips to Master Doodle Jump

This build cycles through four platform behaviors: solid green platforms that never fail, moving platforms that drift sideways, breakable platforms that vanish the instant you bounce off them, and timed platforms that disappear a moment after you land. The game's own code guarantees you never face two breakable platforms in a row, so if you just used one to bounce, the very next breakable one you see is guaranteed safe.

Use screen-wrap to cut corners

Because the play field wraps horizontally, a platform sitting near the left edge is often closer if you drift off the right edge instead of steering all the way across the screen. At higher scores, when moving platforms are common, timing your wrap to land as a platform slides toward you saves the height you would otherwise lose correcting your drift.

This build has no monsters or springs

Unlike the original mobile app, this HTML5 version does not include monsters, black holes, or spring/jetpack power-ups — the entire challenge comes from platform placement and type, so a fall is always the result of missing a jump or landing on a platform that was about to vanish, not an enemy encounter.
